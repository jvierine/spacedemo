Multistage rocket equation

\[ m_{u,i}=m_L+\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}m_{w,j} \] \[ \Delta v_{\mathrm{total}}=g_0\sum_{i=1}^{N} I_{sp,i} \ln\!\left(\frac{m_{w,i}+m_{u,i}}{m_{d,i}+m_{u,i}}\right) \] Stages are numbered from the bottom upward. For stage \(i\), \(m_{w,i}\) is its wet mass before the burn (dry structure plus propellant), \(m_{d,i}\) is its dry mass after its propellant is consumed but before the stage is discarded, and \(m_{u,i}\) is everything above it that remains attached during the burn: the payload plus the wet masses of all upper stages.

Margin

Ideal Δv only; gravity, aerodynamic, steering, residual-propellant, and recovery losses are not included.

Variables

\(\Delta v_{\mathrm{total}}\)
sum of the ideal velocity increments from all stages
\(N\)
number of stages
\(I_{sp,i}\)
specific impulse of stage \(i\)
\(g_0\)
standard gravitational acceleration
\(m_{w,i}\)
wet mass of stage \(i\): dry mass plus propellant
\(m_{d,i}\)
dry mass of stage \(i\), before it is discarded
\(m_{u,i}\)
payload and wet upper-stage mass carried by stage \(i\)
\(m_L\)
payload mass

References and model inputs

  1. NASA Glenn, “Ideal Rocket Equation” — momentum derivation of the rocket equation and the relation \(V_{eq}=I_{sp}g_0\).
  2. SpaceX, “Starship” — current V3 dimensions, propellant, engine counts, thrust, and payload.
  3. SpaceX EU Prospectus, 5 June 2026, p. 98 — 5,533 t Starship vehicle mass and 100+ t reusable LEO payload.
  4. SpaceX, “Falcon 9” and “Falcon Heavy” — current dimensions, total masses, engines, and payload capacities.
  5. NASA Historical Data Book, vol. II, table 1-67 — Saturn V stage dimensions, launch weights, and propulsion.
  6. Wikipedia, “SpaceX Starship”, “SpaceX Raptor”, “Falcon 9”, and “Saturn V” — rounded dry-mass and specific-impulse estimates where manufacturer values are unpublished.
