Lambert problem

Choose two positions and a flight time. Lambert’s problem finds the departure and arrival velocities that connect them with a Keplerian orbit.

\((\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\Delta t)\longrightarrow(\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2)\)

Example

Comet intercept

The impact is day 2200. The buttons reproduce the five exercise cases; the continuous control exposes the complete launch window.

Earth–Mars transfer

The circular coplanar model repeats its Earth–Mars launch geometry about every 780 days. Click the pork-chop diagram to search departure day and flight time.

Equations

\(\ddot{\mathbf r}+\dfrac{GM}{r^2}\dfrac{\mathbf r}{r}=0\)

\(\mathbf r(t_1)=\mathbf r_1,\qquad \mathbf r(t_2)=\mathbf r_2\)

\(C_3=\lVert\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_\oplus\rVert^2=v_\infty^2\)

Variables

\(\mathbf r_1\)
heliocentric departure position
\(\mathbf r_2\)
heliocentric arrival position
\(\Delta t\)
time of flight
\(\mathbf v_1\)
required heliocentric departure velocity
\(\mathbf v_2\)
required heliocentric arrival velocity
\(v_\infty\)
velocity relative to the departure or arrival body outside its sphere of influence
\(C_3\)
characteristic energy at departure

Model

The Sun, spacecraft, and comet use two-body propagation. Earth and Mars follow circular coplanar orbits. The comet’s day-2200 position is placed at the modeled Earth and its exercise velocity is used exactly; planetary ephemerides and planetary gravity are omitted.

References

  1. Izzo, “Revisiting Lambert’s problem,” Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy 121 (2015) — modern derivation and solution structure.
  2. Bate, Mueller and White, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics — textbook treatment of Lambert’s problem and interplanetary transfers.
