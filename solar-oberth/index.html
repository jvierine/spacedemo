Solar Oberth maneuver

Compare a direct prograde burn from Earth’s heliocentric orbit with a Project Lyra-type detour: brake at 1 AU, dive close to the Sun, and accelerate where the spacecraft is fastest.

Mission

The excess speed \(v_\infty\) is the asymptotic cruise speed after escaping the Sun, not the speed at the burn.

Why perihelion?

\(\epsilon=\dfrac{v^2}{2}-\dfrac{\mu_\odot}{r},\qquad \Delta\epsilon=v\,\Delta v+\dfrac{(\Delta v)^2}{2}\)

\(v=\sqrt{\mu_\odot\left(\dfrac{2}{r}-\dfrac{1}{a}\right)}\)

For the same energy gain, a prograde impulse needs less Δv when the initial speed \(v\) is high. The model uses instantaneous, tangential burns and two-body solar gravity.

Model limits

Earth’s gravity, launch from Earth’s surface, planetary flybys, finite burn duration, heat-shield mass, target motion, course corrections, and braking at Alpha Centauri are omitted. Cruise time is distance divided by \(v_\infty\).
