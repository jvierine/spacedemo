Solar Oberth maneuver

Compare a direct vector burn from Earth’s heliocentric orbit with a Project Lyra-type detour: brake at 1 AU, dive close to the Sun, and accelerate where the spacecraft is fastest.

Mission

The excess speed \(v_\infty\) is the asymptotic cruise speed after escaping the Sun, not the speed at the burn.

Why perihelion?

\(\epsilon=\dfrac{v^2}{2}-\dfrac{\mu_\odot}{r},\qquad \Delta\epsilon=v\,\Delta v+\dfrac{(\Delta v)^2}{2}\)

\(v=\sqrt{\mu_\odot\left(\dfrac{2}{r}-\dfrac{1}{a}\right)}\)

\(\Delta v_{exit}=\sqrt{v_{\infty,E}^2+\dfrac{2\mu_E}{r_0}}-\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_E}{r_0}}\)

Both routes enter exactly the same target hyperbola, but at different Earth orbital phases. The solar burn is tangential at perihelion; direct injection occurs separately, where the outgoing target hyperbola crosses 1 AU. Burns are instantaneous and the model uses two-body solar gravity.

Model limits

Earth departure uses a patched-conic escape from the selected circular parking orbit. Each route is shown at its own optimal Earth orbital phase, with the parking-orbit burn oriented to supply the required heliocentric excess vector. Launch from Earth’s surface, planetary flybys, finite burn duration, heat-shield mass, target motion, course corrections, and braking at Alpha Centauri are omitted.
