Hall-effect thruster

Follow one cathode electron into the Hall current, through an argon-ionizing collision, and ultimately to the anode. Then follow the new Ar⁺ ion into a charge-neutral plume.

Particle journey

Governing equations

\(V_a-V_c=V_d,\qquad \mathbf E_z=-\nabla_z\phi,\qquad P_d=V_d I_d\)
\(\left.\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}\right|_z\approx0\quad\text{(axisymmetric model)}\)

\(m\dot{\mathbf v}=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\)
\(\omega_{ce}=\dfrac{eB_r}{m_e},\quad r_L=\dfrac{m_ev_\perp}{eB_r},\quad \mathbf v_{E\times B}=\dfrac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}\)

\(e^-+\mathrm{Ar}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ar}^++2e^-\)

\(\tfrac12m_{Ar}v_i^2=eV_d,\qquad I_{sp}=\dfrac{v_i}{g_0}\)

The discharge supply holds the annular anode positive relative to the external cathode. Magnetized electrons have low axial mobility, allowing that applied voltage to produce an axial potential gradient, strongest near the exit. Their tiny gyromotion is superimposed on an azimuthal Hall drift around the annulus; it is not one electron orbit spanning the channel cross-section. The upstream ionization and near-exit acceleration zones overlap. The plasma remains approximately quasineutral.
