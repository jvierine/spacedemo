Hall-effect thruster

Follow one cathode electron into the Hall current, through an argon-ionizing collision, and ultimately to the anode. Then follow the new Ar⁺ ion into a charge-neutral plume.

Particle journey

Governing equations

\(V_a-V_c=V_d,\qquad \mathbf E_z=-\nabla_z\phi,\qquad P_d=V_d I_d\)

\(m\dot{\mathbf v}=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)\)
\(\omega_{ce}=\dfrac{eB_r}{m_e},\quad r_L=\dfrac{m_ev_\perp}{eB_r},\quad \mathbf v_{E\times B}=\dfrac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}\)

\(e^-+\mathrm{Ar}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ar}^++2e^-\)

\(\tfrac12m_{Ar}v_i^2=eV_d,\qquad I_{sp}=\dfrac{v_i}{g_0}\)

The discharge supply holds the annular anode positive relative to the external cathode. Magnetized electrons have low axial mobility, allowing that applied voltage to produce an axial potential gradient, strongest near the exit. During stage 2, the axial cutout and temporary face-on inset show the same bulk azimuthal Hall current, with small gyromotion superimposed in the inset. The upstream ionization and near-exit acceleration zones overlap. The plasma remains approximately quasineutral.

The purple plume is optical line emission after electron-impact excitation of neutral Ar and Ar⁺. It visualizes luminous plasma, not an intrinsic color of the ion charge; unexcited beam ions are not visibly purple.

References

  1. Goebel, Katz and Mikellides, Fundamentals of Electric Propulsion, 2nd ed. — JPL textbook; Hall thrusters, hollow cathodes, ionization, electron transport, and plumes.
  2. NASA Small Spacecraft Systems, “In-Space Propulsion” — axial electric field, radial magnetic field, Hall current, ion acceleration, and plume neutralization.
  3. McDonald, “Electron Trajectories in a Hall Thruster” — Princeton derivation of electron gyromotion and \(E\times B\) drift.
  4. NIST, “Strong Lines of Argon” — observed spectral lines of neutral and ionized argon.
